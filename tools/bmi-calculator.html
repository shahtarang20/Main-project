Understanding Your BMI

Body Mass Index (BMI) is calculated as weight (kg) divided by height (m) squared. It's a widely used screening tool to categorize weight status into underweight, normal weight, overweight, and obese ranges. BMI is a simple, population-level indicator — it doesn't distinguish between muscle and fat mass, so it may not be accurate for athletes, older adults, or during pregnancy.

BMI Categories

Below 18.5: Underweight  |  18.5–24.9: Normal weight  |  25–29.9: Overweight  |  30 and above: Obese

Frequently Asked Questions

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

No. BMI doesn't account for muscle mass, bone density, or body composition, so it may misclassify very muscular individuals as "overweight" or underestimate risk in people with low muscle mass.

Should I use BMI alone to judge my health?

No — BMI is best used as one general indicator alongside other measures like waist circumference, body fat percentage, and guidance from a healthcare provider.

Does this tool work in pounds and feet?

This version uses centimeters and kilograms. You can convert your measurements using our Unit Converter first if needed.

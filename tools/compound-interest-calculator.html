How Compound Interest Works

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the initial principal and the accumulated interest from previous periods — often described as "interest on interest." The formula used here is A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), where P is the principal, r is the annual interest rate, n is the number of times interest compounds per year, and t is the time in years. The more frequently interest compounds, the faster your investment grows.

Frequently Asked Questions

What compounding frequency should I choose?

This depends on your actual investment or account — banks and funds typically state whether interest compounds daily, monthly, quarterly, or annually. Choose the option that matches your real product for the most accurate estimate.

Why does more frequent compounding produce a higher return?

With more frequent compounding, interest is added to the principal more often, so each subsequent interest calculation is based on a slightly larger balance — leading to marginally higher overall growth compared to less frequent compounding at the same nominal rate.

Does this account for taxes or fees?

No — this calculator shows gross growth based on the rate you enter. Real-world returns may be reduced by taxes, account fees, or inflation.
